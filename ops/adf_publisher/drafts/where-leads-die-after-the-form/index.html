Where Leads Die After the Form

A functioning contact form proves that data moved. It does not prove that a lead entered a functioning sales process.

PUBLISHED {{PUBLISHED_LABEL}} · AURORA DIGITAL FOUNDRY
FORM SUBMITTED
≠ LEAD HANDLED.

Many businesses optimize the visible conversion event and stop there. The button works. The confirmation message appears. Analytics records the submission. From the website's point of view, the job is complete.

From the customer's point of view, the important part has only started.

The hidden funnel begins after submit

A lead can die because the notification went to the wrong inbox, the responsible person was unclear, the first response took too long, the context was incomplete, or nobody knew whether a follow-up had already happened.

CaptureName, need, contact details, source.
HandoffRoute, owner, response clock, next state.
Follow-upHistory, reminders, unresolved questions.
OutcomeWon, lost, deferred, referred, or still open.

Conversion is a system, not a button.

Trace one real inquiry

Do not begin with a software shopping list. Take one actual lead and follow it from submission to outcome. Which system touched it? How many times was information re-entered? Where could ownership become ambiguous? What evidence tells the business that the loop is closed?

That trace usually reveals more useful work than another redesign. Sometimes the website is not the bottleneck at all. The leak begins one handoff later.
